Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 · Practical skills

Presentations

The presentation task is mostly about consistency. Almost every mark comes from doing something once on the master slide so that it appears identically on every slide, and from following the instructions about layout, notes, transitions and printing exactly.

Syllabus coverage: 19 Presentations - create a presentation, use a master slide, edit a presentation, output the presentation
19.1

Creating a Presentation

Creating a new presentation from a text file

  • A plain .txt or .rtf file can be imported to create the slides automatically.
  • The indentation levels in the text file decide the structure: text at the left margin becomes a slide title, the first level of indent becomes a first-level bullet, the second level becomes a sub-bullet, and so on.
  • Importing is far faster than typing, and it removes the risk of typing errors in the content.
  • After importing, check that every slide has been created and that no bullet has ended up at the wrong level.
19.2

Using a Master Slide

What a master slide is for

A master slide holds the formatting and objects that are to appear on every slide. Anything placed on the master appears automatically on all slides, in exactly the same position.
  • Guarantees consistency - every slide has identical fonts, colours and logo placement.
  • Saves a great deal of time - one change updates the whole presentation.
  • Supports the organisation's house style.
  • In the exam, "must appear on every slide" is an instruction to use the master slide, not to copy and paste onto each slide.

Inserting and editing objects on the master slide

Insert and edit objects consistently, including:

  • Images and logos, placed in the same position on every slide
  • Text such as a company name or a candidate's details
  • Shapes and lines used as decoration or dividers
  • Slide headers and footers
  • Placeholder position - where the title and content boxes sit on the slide
  • Automated slide numbering, which renumbers itself if slides are added or moved

Formatting master slide objects

  • Headings - font face, size, colour and alignment of the slide title
  • Subheadings - formatted so they are clearly subordinate to the heading
  • Bullets - the shape, size, colour and indent of each bullet level
  • Background colour - chosen to contrast strongly with the text so it is readable from the back of a room
Exam tip: if an object must not appear on the title slide, that is usually a separate instruction - check whether the question wants the master applied to every slide or to all but the first.
Check your understanding
A logo must appear in the same place on all 20 slides. Explain the best way to do it.
Place the logo once on the master slide in the required position. It then appears automatically on every slide in exactly the same place, and if it has to be moved or replaced later only one change is needed. Pasting it onto each slide separately is slow and the positions would not match.
19.3

Editing a Presentation

Working with slides

  • Apply slide layout - title slide, title and content, two content, blank, and so on.
  • Insert a new slide, move a slide to a different position, and delete a slide.
  • Hide slides within a presentation - the slide stays in the file and can still be reached by a hyperlink or action button, but it is skipped during a normal slide show. This is used to keep extra detail available in case it is asked for, without showing it every time.

Inserting and editing objects on a slide

  • Text - headings, subheadings and bulleted lists
  • Images - still images, video clips and animated images
  • Charts and tables
  • Audio clips (sound)
  • Symbols, lines, arrows, call out boxes and shapes
Slide design rules that earn marks: few words per bullet, no more than about six bullets per slide, a font large enough to read from the back of the room, and strong contrast between text and background.

Notes, links and accessibility

  • Add presenter notes - the extra detail the speaker says, visible to the presenter and printable, but not shown to the audience during the show.
  • Insert and edit a hyperlink from text or an object to:
    • a slide within the presentation
    • an external file
    • an email address
  • Insert an action button and modify its settings so it navigates to a specified slide or file - used for menus and for jumping to hidden slides.
  • Add alternative text or a screentip to an object. Alternative text describes the object for screen readers used by visually impaired viewers, and is displayed if the image cannot load; a screentip appears when the pointer hovers over the object.

Transitions and animations

Transitions
  • The effect used when moving from one slide to the next
  • Must be applied consistently - the same transition throughout unless told otherwise
  • Can be set to occur on a click or automatically after a set time
Animations
  • The effect applied to objects on a slide - text, images and other objects
  • Used to reveal bullets one at a time so the audience listens rather than reads ahead
  • Must also be applied consistently across the presentation
Transitions move between slides; animations move things on a slide. Overusing either distracts the audience from the content.
Check your understanding
State the difference between presenter notes and a slide's content.
The slide content is what the audience sees and should be brief. Presenter notes are the additional detail the speaker wants to say; they are visible only to the presenter and can be printed, but they do not appear on the projected slide.
Why add alternative text to an image?
So that a screen reader can describe the image to a visually impaired viewer, and so that a description is still shown if the image fails to display. It makes the presentation accessible to a wider audience.
19.4

Outputting the Presentation

Displaying the presentation

Looped on-screen carousel
  • Runs automatically and continuously, restarting when it reaches the end
  • Timings are set for each slide, so no one has to click
  • Used for unattended displays at exhibitions, receptions and shop windows
Presenter controlled
  • The speaker advances each slide manually with a click or a remote control
  • The pace can be matched to the audience, and the speaker can pause for questions
  • Presenter view shows the notes and the next slide to the speaker only

Printing the presentation

LayoutWhat it printsUsed for
Full page slidesOne slide per page, at full sizeChecking the design in detail; overhead copies
Presenter notesA small image of the slide with its notes underneathThe speaker's own copy to talk from
HandoutsSeveral slides per page, often with lines for writingCopies for the audience to take away and annotate
Exam habit: the question always states the print layout and often the number of slides per page. Print exactly what is asked for - a handout printed as full page slides scores nothing.
Check your understanding
A company wants a display running unattended in its reception all day. Which output method should it use and why?
A looped on-screen carousel. Slide timings are set so the presentation advances automatically and restarts at the end, so it runs continuously without anyone having to click through it.
Presentations checklist
  • I can create a presentation from a text file, using indentation to set titles and bullet levels.
  • I can use a master slide for images, logos, text, shapes, headers and footers, placeholders and automated slide numbering.
  • I can format master slide headings, subheadings, bullets and background colour.
  • I can apply slide layouts, and insert, move, delete and hide slides.
  • I can insert and edit text, images, video, animated images, charts, tables, audio, symbols, lines, arrows, call out boxes and shapes.
  • I can add presenter notes, hyperlinks to a slide, a file or an email address, and action buttons.
  • I can add alternative text and screentips, and explain why they matter.
  • I can apply consistent transitions between slides and consistent animations to objects.
  • I can output the presentation as a looped carousel or presenter controlled, and print full page slides, presenter notes and handouts.
