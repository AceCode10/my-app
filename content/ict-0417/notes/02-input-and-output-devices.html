Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 · Topic 2

Input and Output Devices

For every device in this topic you need four things: its characteristics, its uses, its advantages and its disadvantages. Learn them as a set of four and you can answer almost any question in this topic.

Syllabus coverage: 2.1 Input devices and their uses · 2.2 Direct data entry and associated devices · 2.3 Output devices and their uses
2.1

Input Devices and Their Uses

Manual input devices

Keyboard Input
  • Characteristics: keys are pressed to enter text, numbers and commands; layout is usually QWERTY; may be wired, wireless or on-screen
Uses
  • Entering text into documents, emails and databases
  • Entering commands and using keyboard shortcuts
Advantages
  • Easy to use; most people are familiar with it
  • Fast for experienced typists
  • Easy to verify data as it appears on the screen
Disadvantages
  • Slow for large volumes of data compared with direct data entry
  • Typing errors are easily made
  • Prolonged use can cause repetitive strain injury (RSI)
  • Takes up desk space
Numeric keypad Input
  • Characteristics: a small set of number keys only, often with a few function keys
Uses
  • ATMs, chip and PIN readers and electronic point of sale terminals
  • Telephones and mobile phones
  • Entering numeric data quickly into a spreadsheet
Advantages
  • Faster than a full keyboard for entering numbers
  • Very small, so it fits into portable devices
Disadvantages
  • Keys can be small and difficult to use
  • Sometimes difficult to see the numbers in poor light
  • Cannot enter text easily
Pointing devices: mouse, trackerball, touchpad Input
  • Characteristics: movement of the hand is translated into movement of an on-screen pointer; buttons select, open and drag
  • A trackerball is a ball rotated by the hand and stays still on the desk; a touchpad senses a finger sliding across a surface
Uses
  • Selecting icons, menus and buttons in a GUI
  • Drawing and editing images in graphics software
  • Control rooms and medical settings, where a trackerball is used because it does not need a flat surface
Advantages
  • Faster than a keyboard for selecting options
  • Very easy to learn and use
  • A trackerball needs no desk space and causes less wrist strain
Disadvantages
  • Difficult for users with limited hand control
  • A mouse needs a flat surface and can be damaged easily
  • Prolonged use can cause RSI
Remote control Input
  • Characteristics: handheld device that sends infrared signals to control equipment from a distance
Uses
  • Operating televisions, set-top boxes, air conditioning and home entertainment systems
  • Controlling multimedia systems and projectors during a presentation
  • Operating equipment in hazardous conditions from a safe distance
Advantages
  • Equipment can be operated from a distance, which helps users with limited mobility
  • Allows control of devices in dangerous areas without going near them
Disadvantages
  • Difficult for people with limited hand movement to use the small buttons
  • The signal can be blocked by obstacles - it needs line of sight
  • Only works over a short range
Joystick and driving wheel Input
  • Characteristics: a lever or wheel moved by hand; movement in two or three directions is sent to the computer, often with force feedback
Uses
  • Video games, especially flight and driving games
  • Flight simulators and driving simulators used for training
  • Controlling machinery such as cranes and remotely operated vehicles
Advantages
  • Easier than a keyboard for controlling movement in games
  • Driving wheels give a more realistic experience, improving training
Disadvantages
  • More difficult to control an on-screen pointer than with a mouse
  • Can be expensive, and simulators need a lot of space
Touch screen (as an input device) Input
  • Characteristics: the user makes a selection by touching the display directly; resistive, capacitive and infrared types exist
Uses
  • Smartphones, tablets and satellite navigation systems
  • Self-service tills, ticket machines, ATMs and information kiosks
  • Ordering systems in restaurants
Advantages
  • Very easy and quick to use - no training needed
  • No extra keyboard or mouse, so it saves space
  • Choices can be limited to valid options, reducing errors
  • Tamper-proof, which suits public places
Disadvantages
  • Limited number of options can be shown at once
  • The screen gets dirty with fingerprints and can spread germs
  • Can be tiring to use for long periods, and wears out with heavy use
Light pen Input
  • Characteristics: a pen-shaped device containing a light sensor, held against the screen to select or draw
Uses
  • Selecting objects on screen in CAD packages
  • Drawing directly on the screen, and marking up designs
Advantages
  • More accurate than a finger on a touch screen
  • Small, easy to use and takes up little space
Disadvantages
  • Problems with lag when drawing on screen
  • Only works with certain types of monitor
  • Rather dated technology with limited drawing accuracy

Data capture devices

Scanners (2D and 3D) Input
  • Characteristics: a hard-copy document or object is converted into a digital image; a 3D scanner captures the shape of a solid object
Uses
  • Scanning documents and photographs into a computer
  • Scanning old and fragile documents so the originals are not handled
  • 3D scanning for medical use and to produce a model for a 3D printer
Advantages
  • Images can be stored, edited and shared electronically
  • Faster and more accurate than retyping a document
  • Damaged originals can be restored digitally
Disadvantages
  • Quality is limited by the resolution of the scanner
  • Scanned images produce large files
  • Scanning many pages is slow, and text still needs OCR before it can be edited
Digital camera Input
  • Characteristics: captures still images or video and stores them on solid-state media as digital files; many are built into phones and tablets
Uses
  • Taking photographs and video for documents, websites and social media
  • Video calls and video-conferencing using a webcam
  • Recording evidence, for example in insurance or surveying
Advantages
  • No film or developing costs; poor photographs can be deleted immediately
  • Images are already digital, so they can be edited and sent at once
  • Very high quality is now available at low cost
Disadvantages
  • Some skill is needed to get the best results and to edit images
  • Large image files fill up memory cards and need compressing
  • Batteries run down, and the images may need transferring before use
Microphone Input
  • Characteristics: converts sound waves into an electrical signal; an ADC then converts it into digital data the computer can store
Uses
  • Recording sound for podcasts, presentations and video
  • Voice over Internet Protocol (VoIP) calls and video-conferencing
  • Speech recognition, dialogue based interfaces and voice-activated controls in cars
Advantages
  • Faster to input speech than to type it
  • Helps users with a disability that makes typing difficult
  • Allows hands-free operation, which improves safety when driving
Disadvantages
  • Sound files can be very large
  • Speech recognition is inaccurate with accents, unusual words or background noise
  • Recordings can be altered, so they may not be accepted as evidence
Sensors Input
  • Characteristics: read a physical quantity continuously and produce an analogue signal, which an ADC converts for the computer
  • Types: temperature, pressure, light, sound, humidity, moisture, pH, infrared, motion, gas, magnetic field
Uses
  • Monitoring systems, such as a pollution monitor or a patient monitor, which only report readings
  • Control systems, such as a greenhouse, central heating or an automatic door, which act on the readings
  • Burglar alarms and automatic street lights
Advantages
  • Readings are more accurate than a human would take
  • Readings can be taken continuously, 24 hours a day
  • The system reacts instantly and never forgets or gets tired
  • Can work in places that are dangerous for people
Disadvantages
  • Faulty sensors give wrong readings, so the system reacts wrongly
  • Sensors and the ADC add cost, and need calibrating and maintaining
  • Systems cannot use judgement if something unexpected happens
Check your understanding
Why is an ADC needed between a temperature sensor and a microprocessor?
The sensor produces a continuously varying analogue signal, but a microprocessor can only process digital data. The analogue to digital converter turns the signal into digital values the processor can use.
Give two reasons why a museum ticket desk uses a touch screen rather than a keyboard and mouse.
A touch screen is easy for any visitor to use with no training, and it takes up less space than a separate keyboard and mouse. It is also tamper-proof and can be limited to valid choices, which reduces errors.
2.2

Direct Data Entry and Associated Devices

What direct data entry means

Direct data entry (DDE) means data is read straight into the computer from a source document or card without being typed in by a person.
  • Much faster than manual entry when there is a lot of data
  • Far more accurate, because there are no typing errors
  • Fewer staff are needed, so running costs fall
  • But the equipment costs more to buy, and unreadable or damaged items are rejected

The direct data entry devices

Magnetic stripe reader Direct data entry
  • Characteristics: reads data held in the magnetic stripe on the back of a card as it is swiped through the reader
Uses
  • Credit and debit cards, and older bank cards
  • Security badges and hotel room key cards
  • Travel and season tickets, and library cards
Advantages
  • Fast and error-free compared with typing the number in
  • Cheap, simple technology; the data is not human-readable, so it is more secure than printing
  • Cards can be reprogrammed and reused
Disadvantages
  • The stripe holds only a small amount of data
  • Can be damaged or wiped by a strong magnetic field or scratching
  • The card must be physically swiped, so it will not work at a distance
  • Stripes can be cloned, which is a fraud risk
Chip and PIN reader Direct data entry
  • Characteristics: reads an embedded chip in the card; the customer must key in a personal identification number (PIN) which is checked against the chip
Uses
  • Paying for goods at EFTPOS terminals in shops and restaurants
  • Withdrawing cash from ATMs
Advantages
  • More secure than a magnetic stripe - the chip is encrypted and much harder to clone
  • The PIN proves the person using the card is the owner
  • Portable readers let the customer keep the card in sight
Disadvantages
  • The PIN can be observed by shoulder surfing
  • The customer must remember the PIN
  • Readers are more expensive than magnetic stripe readers
Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) reader Direct data entry
  • Characteristics: a reader sends out radio waves that power a tag; the tag sends back its stored data, with no line of sight needed
Uses
  • Tracking stock in warehouses and shops
  • Passports and identity documents
  • Tracking automobiles on a production line and on toll roads
  • Contactless payment, and livestock and pet identification
Advantages
  • Many tags can be read at once, and no line of sight is needed
  • Very fast and robust - tags work through packaging
  • Tags can be read and written, so data can be updated
Disadvantages
  • Tags and readers are more expensive than bar codes
  • Signals can be blocked or jammed by metal and liquids
  • Tags can be read without the owner knowing, which raises privacy concerns
  • Tag collision can occur when several tags reply at the same time
Optical Mark Recognition / Reader (OMR) Direct data entry
  • Characteristics: detects the position of a mark made in a pre-printed box on a form - it does not read what the mark is
Uses
  • Multiple-choice examination papers
  • School registers and attendance sheets
  • Questionnaires, surveys and lottery tickets
Advantages
  • Very fast - thousands of forms can be read in a short time
  • More accurate than typing the data in by hand
  • Fewer staff are needed to process the forms
Disadvantages
  • Forms must be designed carefully and printed accurately, which is expensive
  • Marks in the wrong place or not dark enough are rejected or misread
  • Creased or folded forms may jam the reader
  • Only suitable for choices, not for free text
Optical Character Recognition / Reader (OCR) Direct data entry
  • Characteristics: scans printed or handwritten characters and converts them into editable text by matching shapes to known characters
Uses
  • Automatic number plate recognition (ANPR) in car parks, toll roads and speed enforcement
  • Converting scanned paper documents into editable word-processed files
  • Processing passports at automated border control
Advantages
  • Much faster than retyping a document
  • The text becomes editable and searchable
  • Fewer staff needed, so costs are lower
Disadvantages
  • Not fully accurate, especially with poor print or handwriting
  • Work must still be proofread carefully
  • Formatting of the original document is often lost
Bar code reader Direct data entry
  • Characteristics: a laser or LED reads a pattern of parallel lines of different widths; the number read is looked up in the stock file to find the price and description
  • The bar code itself holds a code, not the price - so prices only need changing in one place
Uses
  • Supermarket and shop point of sale terminals
  • Library book issue and return systems
  • Tracking parcels and luggage, and stock control in warehouses
Advantages
  • Much faster and more accurate than keying in a product number
  • Prices are changed once on the computer, not on every item
  • Stock levels are updated automatically, allowing automatic reordering
  • Cheap to print
Disadvantages
  • Needs line of sight and only one code can be read at a time
  • Damaged, creased or dirty bar codes cannot be read
  • Holds only a small amount of data
  • The system is expensive to set up
QR scanner Direct data entry
  • Characteristics: reads a two-dimensional matrix of black and white squares; usually scanned with a smartphone camera
Uses
  • Advertising, so a poster can link straight to a website
  • Tickets, boarding passes and event entry
  • Contactless menus and payment links
Advantages
  • Holds far more data than a bar code, including web addresses and text
  • Can be read from any angle, and works even if partly damaged because of error correction
  • Any smartphone can read one - no special hardware needed
Disadvantages
  • The user cannot see where the code leads, so a malicious QR code can send them to a fake website
  • A smartphone and an internet connection are usually needed
  • More than one QR code standard exists
Check your understanding
Explain why a school uses OMR rather than OCR for multiple-choice examination papers.
OMR only needs to detect the position of a mark in a printed box, which is very fast and highly accurate. OCR would have to recognise the shapes of characters, which is slower and much less reliable with handwriting.
Give two advantages of RFID over bar codes for stock control.
No line of sight is needed, so goods can be read inside boxes and pallets, and many tags can be read at once, which is far faster. RFID tags can also be rewritten, so stock data can be updated on the tag.
2.3

Output Devices and Their Uses

Screens and projectors

Monitor Output
  • Characteristics: produces a temporary soft copy on screen; modern monitors use LCD panels with LED backlighting, or OLED panels that light each pixel themselves
Uses
  • Showing the output of almost any application as the user works
  • Displaying photographs, video and CAD drawings
  • Public information displays and advertising screens
Advantages
  • Output is immediate and can be changed easily
  • No paper or ink is used, so no running costs and no waste
  • Thin, light and low power compared with older CRT screens
  • Can show moving images and sound-linked content
Disadvantages
  • Output is temporary - it is lost when the screen changes or the power goes off
  • Only one or two people can read it comfortably at a time
  • Prolonged viewing can cause eye strain and headaches
Touch screen (as an output device) Output
  • Characteristics: the same panel both displays the output and accepts input by touch, so it is an input and an output device
Uses
  • Smartphones, tablets and satellite navigation systems
  • ATMs, self-service checkouts and ticket machines
  • Interactive information points in museums and airports
Advantages
  • Saves space, because no separate keyboard, mouse or monitor is needed
  • Very intuitive - the user touches what they can see
  • The display can change to show only the valid options
Disadvantages
  • Fingerprints and dirt reduce the quality of the display
  • The screen can be damaged or worn by heavy public use
  • Small screens are difficult to use accurately
Multimedia projector Output
  • Characteristics: takes output from a computer or player and projects a large image onto a screen or wall
Uses
  • Training sessions, business presentations and conferences
  • Classroom teaching, often with an interactive whiteboard
  • Home cinema and advertising displays
Advantages
  • A large audience can see the output at once
  • Avoids everyone crowding around a small monitor
  • Portable models can be moved between rooms
Disadvantages
  • Images can be fuzzy, and the room usually has to be darkened
  • Expensive to buy, and replacement bulbs are costly
  • Setting up can be difficult, and the fans are noisy

Printers and plotters

Laser printer Output
  • Characteristics: uses a laser and a charged drum to attract powdered toner, which is then fused to the paper by heat; prints a whole page at a time
Uses
  • High-volume printing in offices and schools
  • Printing where noise must be kept low
  • Printing bills, payslips and mailshots
Advantages
  • Very fast for large print jobs
  • Quiet in operation and very reliable
  • Large toner cartridges give a low cost per page
  • Large paper trays mean less refilling
Disadvantages
  • Expensive to buy, especially colour models
  • Produces ozone and toner particles, so good ventilation is needed
  • Colour printing is more expensive than mono
Inkjet printer Output
  • Characteristics: sprays tiny droplets of liquid ink onto the paper from a moving print head; prints line by line
Uses
  • Home and small office printing
  • Printing high-quality photographs
  • Short print runs where quality matters more than speed
Advantages
  • Cheap to buy and compact
  • Very high quality output, especially for photographs
  • Quiet, and does not produce ozone
Disadvantages
  • Slow for large print jobs, and the small ink cartridges run out quickly
  • High cost per page - ink is expensive
  • The ink can smudge if it is not allowed to dry, and nozzles clog if unused
  • Small paper trays need frequent refilling
Dot matrix printer Output
  • Characteristics: an impact printer - pins strike an inked ribbon against the paper, so it can print through multi-part stationery
Uses
  • Noisy, dusty or damp environments such as a factory, garage or warehouse
  • Printing multi-part forms, such as invoices with carbon copies
  • Continuous printing of logs, for example at a weighbridge
Advantages
  • Can be used in damp and dusty conditions where other printers would fail
  • Carbon copies and multi-part stationery can be printed
  • Very cheap to run and extremely robust
Disadvantages
  • Very noisy, so unsuitable for an office
  • Poor print quality and very slow
  • Colour printing is very limited
(Graph) plotter Output
  • Characteristics: draws continuous lines using pens, or cuts material with a blade; works on very large sheets
Uses
  • Producing architectural, engineering and CAD drawings
  • Printing large posters, banners and billboards
  • Cutting vinyl lettering and signs
Advantages
  • Can print on very large sheets of paper, far bigger than A3
  • Very high quality, accurate continuous lines
  • Can print or cut on materials such as card, vinyl and plastic
Disadvantages
  • Very slow, and expensive to buy and maintain
  • Takes up a lot of space
  • Not suitable for everyday text printing
3D printer Output
  • Characteristics: builds a solid object layer by layer from a CAD model, using plastic, resin, metal powder or even living cells
Uses
  • Prototypes for product designers and engineers
  • Medical use: prosthetics, tissue engineering, artificial blood vessels, customised medicines
  • Spare parts made on demand, and precision models in aerospace and construction
Advantages
  • Prototypes and one-off items can be made quickly and cheaply
  • Objects can be customised to an individual, such as a prosthetic limb
  • Parts can be produced where they are needed, cutting transport
  • Complex shapes that are impossible to machine can be produced
Disadvantages
  • Printers and materials are expensive, and printing is slow
  • Could be used to produce dangerous items such as weapons, or to breach copyright
  • Job losses in traditional manufacturing
  • Objects may be weaker than those made by traditional methods

Sound and movement

Speakers Output
  • Characteristics: convert an electrical signal into sound; a DAC converts the computer's digital sound data into the analogue signal they need
Uses
  • Playing music, video soundtracks and games audio
  • Alarms and warning sounds in control systems
  • Text-to-speech output for visually impaired users, and public address announcements
Advantages
  • Output can be heard by several people at once without looking at a screen
  • Helps visually impaired users, and works when the user's eyes are busy
  • Warnings attract attention immediately
Disadvantages
  • Sound quality is limited by the size and cost of the speakers
  • Can disturb other people, so headphones may be needed
  • No use to a user with a hearing impairment, and output is temporary
Actuators Output
  • Characteristics: a transducer that turns an electrical signal into movement; it operates motors, pumps, valves, switches and heaters in a control system
  • A DAC is needed because the actuator responds to an analogue signal
Uses
  • Opening windows and switching heaters in an automatic greenhouse
  • Operating robot arms on a production line
  • Opening automatic doors, raising barriers and controlling valves in a chemical plant
Advantages
  • Allows a computer to control physical devices automatically, without a person
  • Reacts immediately and works continuously without tiring
  • Can operate in dangerous conditions where a person could not
Disadvantages
  • A faulty actuator stops the whole control system working
  • Needs regular maintenance, and adds cost to the system
  • Extra hardware, such as a DAC, is required
Check your understanding
A car repair garage needs to print job sheets in triplicate in a dusty workshop. Which printer should it use and why?
A dot matrix printer. It is an impact printer, so it can print through multi-part stationery to give carbon copies, and it is robust enough to work in dusty and damp conditions where an inkjet or laser printer would fail.
Give two reasons why a school office chooses a laser printer rather than an inkjet.
A laser printer is much faster for large print runs such as reports and letters, and its large toner cartridges give a much lower cost per page. It is also quieter and more reliable, with larger paper trays.
Name the output device used in an automatic greenhouse to open a window, and state why a DAC is needed.
An actuator (driving a motor) opens the window. The computer produces a digital signal, but the actuator needs an analogue signal to operate, so a digital to analogue converter is required.

  • I can describe magnetic stripe, chip and PIN, RFID, OMR, OCR, bar code and QR technologies and say where each is used.
