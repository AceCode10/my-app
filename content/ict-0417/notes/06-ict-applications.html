6.1

Communication

Communication media

Newsletters
  • Characteristics: a short, regular publication, printed or emailed, with a house style, headings, columns and images
Uses
  • Keeping members, staff, customers or parents up to date
  • Advertising events and promotions to an existing audience
Posters
  • Characteristics: a single large sheet designed to be read from a distance - few words, large text, one strong image
Uses
  • Advertising an event, product or campaign in a public place
  • Safety and health information notices
Websites
  • Characteristics: pages of text, images, sound and video linked by hyperlinks; can be updated instantly and reached worldwide; can include interactivity such as forms and searches
Uses
  • Providing information and selling to a global audience
  • Online learning, news and support
Advantages
  • Reaches a worldwide audience at low cost
  • Can be updated immediately and includes sound and video
  • Hyperlinks let readers find what they want quickly
Disadvantages
  • The reader needs a device and an internet connection
  • Needs skill and money to design, host and maintain
  • Can be hacked, and information may be copied
Multimedia presentations
  • Characteristics: slides combining text, images, sound, video, animations, transitions and hyperlinks; can be presenter-controlled or looped as a carousel
Uses
  • Business pitches, training and classroom teaching
  • Unattended displays at exhibitions and receptions
Audio and video
  • Characteristics: recorded sound or moving images distributed as files; video adds visual demonstration, audio can be listened to while doing something else
Uses
  • Podcasts, radio programmes and audio books
  • Training videos, product demonstrations and online tutorials
Media streaming
  • Characteristics: the file is played as it is delivered rather than being downloaded first; a small buffer stores the next few seconds
Uses
  • Watching films and television on demand
  • Listening to music services and live radio
  • Live streaming of events and lessons
Advantages
  • No need to wait for a full download or to store the file
  • Content can be watched live, and can be stopped at any time
  • Saves storage space on the user's device
Disadvantages
  • Needs a fast, reliable connection - it buffers or drops quality if the connection is slow
  • Uses a large amount of data, which may be costly on mobile
  • Nothing can be watched offline unless it is downloaded separately
ePublications
  • Characteristics: books, magazines and newspapers published electronically and read on a computer, tablet or e-reader; text can be searched, resized and read aloud
Uses
  • eBooks, digital magazines, online newspapers and academic journals
  • School textbooks with embedded video and self-marking questions
Advantages
  • Many titles are carried on one light device
  • Cheaper to produce and distribute - no paper, printing or transport
  • Text can be enlarged or read aloud, helping visually impaired readers
  • Can be updated and bought instantly, anywhere
Disadvantages
  • Needs a device with battery power
  • Reading a screen for long periods causes eye strain
  • Easy to copy illegally, which affects authors' income

Mobile communication

SMS (text) messaging
  • Short text messages sent over the mobile phone network
  • Cheap, quick and read almost immediately
  • Works on any phone and on a weak signal
  • Limited length, and no guarantee the message has been read
Phone calls
  • Real-time voice conversation over the mobile network
  • Immediate, and tone of voice conveys meaning
  • Both people must be free at the same time, and there is no written record
Voice over Internet Protocol (VoIP)
  • Voice is converted to digital data and sent over the internet rather than the phone network
  • Much cheaper, especially for international calls, and often free between users of the same service
  • Needs a good broadband connection; quality drops if the connection is poor, and it fails in a power cut
Video calls
  • Sound and live video between two or more people, using a camera and microphone
  • Body language and demonstrations can be shared; screens and files can be shown
  • Needs a fast connection and more bandwidth than audio alone
Accessing the internet
  • Mobile devices connect through wi-fi or the mobile phone network
  • Email, web, maps, banking and social media are available anywhere
  • Mobile data can be expensive and the signal is not available everywhere
Check your understanding
A school wants to tell parents about a change of date today. Compare a newsletter with an SMS message.
An SMS is better here: it is delivered and read almost immediately, is cheap, and works on any phone. A newsletter takes time to write, print and distribute, so it would arrive too late, although it can carry much more detail and images.
Explain why a family in one country uses VoIP to call relatives in another.
VoIP sends the call as data over the internet, so there are no international call charges - it is usually free or very cheap between users of the same service, and video can be added. The drawback is that both ends need a reliable broadband connection.
6.2

Modelling Applications

What computer modelling is

A computer model is a computer program that represents a real-life situation mathematically, so that a user can ask "what if...?" questions and see the predicted results without changing anything in the real world.
  • Data about the real situation is collected and rules are written to describe how it behaves.
  • Variables are changed and the model is run to produce a prediction.
  • Results are compared with what really happens, and the model is refined.
Simulation is a model that is run to imitate the real situation over time, often with output that looks and feels real - for example a flight simulator.

Examples of models you must know

Personal finance
  • A spreadsheet model of income, spending, savings and loan repayments
  • "What if my rent rises by 10%?" or "What if I overpay the loan by $50 a month?"
  • Used by individuals to budget and by banks to test whether a loan is affordable
Bridge and building design
  • A CAD model tested against forces such as weight, wind, traffic load and earthquakes
  • Materials and dimensions are changed until the design is safe and economical
  • Avoids building an expensive physical prototype that might fail
Flood water management
  • Uses data on rainfall, river levels, tides, ground type and land height
  • Predicts where and when flooding will happen so warnings can be issued
  • Tests the effect of proposed flood defences before they are built
Traffic management
  • Uses data from induction loops, cameras and sensors counting vehicles
  • Tests the effect of changing traffic light timings, adding a lane or closing a road
  • Reduces congestion, journey time and pollution without disrupting real traffic
Weather forecasting
  • Data from weather stations, balloons, ships and satellites feeds a mathematical model of the atmosphere
  • Predicts temperature, rainfall, wind and storm tracks days ahead
  • Allows severe weather warnings, saving lives and property

Advantages and disadvantages of modelling rather than using humans

Advantages
  • Safer - dangerous situations such as a bridge collapse or a nuclear accident can be tested without risk to life
  • Cheaper than building real prototypes, and mistakes cost nothing
  • Much faster - years of change can be modelled in seconds, or slowed down to study detail
  • Variables can be changed easily and the model rerun as often as needed
  • Situations that are impossible to try in real life can be investigated, such as a once-in-a-century flood
Disadvantages
  • The model is only as good as the data and the rules put into it - wrong data gives wrong predictions
  • Real situations are complex and the model is always a simplification
  • Expensive to develop, and it needs powerful computers and skilled staff
  • People may trust the results without question
  • Unexpected human behaviour is very hard to model
Check your understanding
Give two reasons why a city council models a new road layout before building it.
Modelling is far cheaper than building the road and finding it does not work, and it is faster - the effect of many different layouts can be tested in hours. It also avoids disrupting real traffic during the trial.
Why are weather forecasts sometimes wrong even though powerful computers are used?
The model is a simplification of an extremely complex system, and it can only use the data available from weather stations and satellites. If that data is incomplete or slightly inaccurate, or if the rules do not capture every factor, the prediction will be wrong.
6.3

Computer Controlled Systems

Examples

Robotics in manufacture
  • Robot arms with sensors, motors and grippers
  • Welding, painting, assembling and packing
  • Programmed by leading the arm through the movements or by writing instructions
Production line control
  • Sensors monitor speed, temperature, weight and position along the line
  • The computer controls conveyors, fillers and rejection gates
  • Faulty items are removed automatically
Autonomous vehicles
  • Cameras, radar, lidar, GPS and ultrasonic sensors read the surroundings
  • The processor decides on speed, steering and braking
  • Actuators operate the steering, accelerator and brakes

Advantages and disadvantages of computer controlled systems rather than humans

Advantages
  • Work 24 hours a day without breaks, holidays or pay
  • More consistent and accurate - every product is identical and quality is higher
  • Faster production and higher output, so unit costs fall
  • Can work in dangerous or unpleasant conditions - heat, chemicals, heavy lifting
  • Do not get tired, bored or ill, so fewer mistakes are made
  • Easily reprogrammed when the product changes
Disadvantages
  • Unemployment - workers are replaced and may need retraining
  • Very high set-up costs for equipment, installation and programming
  • Systems cannot use judgement or deal with a situation they were not programmed for
  • A breakdown or power cut stops the whole line, and specialist engineers are needed
  • Factories can become 'de-skilled', and traditional skills are lost
Check your understanding
Give two reasons why a car factory uses robots for paint spraying.
Paint spraying involves harmful fumes, so a robot removes the health risk to workers. Robots also apply the paint with exactly the same thickness every time, giving consistent quality, and they can work continuously without breaks.
State one disadvantage to the workforce and one to the company.
Workforce: job losses, with staff needing to retrain for different work. Company: very high initial cost of buying, installing and programming the robots, and the need for specialist maintenance engineers.
6.4

School Management Systems

Learner registration and attendance

  • Registration is taken electronically, using OMR forms, magnetic stripe cards, smart cards, RFID tags or biometrics such as a fingerprint.
  • The system records who is present, absent or late, lesson by lesson.
  • Absence patterns are spotted automatically and reports produced for staff.
  • Parents can be sent an automatic text or email when a learner is absent.
Advantages
  • Much faster than a paper register, saving teaching time
  • Data is accurate and cannot be altered by learners
  • Attendance reports and trends are produced automatically
  • An instant list of who is in the building is available in an emergency
Disadvantages
  • Expensive to buy, install and maintain
  • Staff need training, and the system fails if the network or power fails
  • Personal and biometric data must be protected, or it could be misused
  • Cards can be lost, swapped or forgotten

Recording learner performance and computer aided learning

  • Test and examination marks, coursework grades and predicted grades are stored centrally.
  • Progress is tracked over time and compared with targets; reports for parents are generated automatically.
  • Computer aided learning (CAL) delivers lessons, quizzes and revision material through software.
  • CAL marks work automatically, gives instant feedback and adapts the difficulty to the learner.
Advantages
  • Learners work at their own pace, repeating material as often as they need
  • Instant marking and feedback, available at any time of day
  • Teachers see at a glance which learners need help
  • Multimedia makes some topics easier to understand
Disadvantages
  • Less contact with a teacher, and questions outside the program cannot be answered
  • Learners need self-discipline, and there is little social interaction
  • Expensive to buy the software and the hardware to run it
  • Practical skills cannot be taught this way
Check your understanding
Give two benefits to a school of an electronic registration system.
Registration is much quicker, so less teaching time is lost, and attendance reports and absence patterns are produced automatically. The school also has an immediate list of everyone in the building if there is a fire.
6.5

Online Booking Systems

How an online booking system works

  • The customer searches for a date, destination, film or event on a website or app.
  • The system searches the database and shows the seats or places still available.
  • The customer selects a seat; the record is flagged as unavailable straight away so nobody else can book it.
  • Payment is taken electronically and the record is updated to 'booked'.
  • A confirmation, e-ticket or booking reference is emailed or sent to the app.
The key idea is that the database is updated in real time, which is what prevents double booking.
Uses
  • Travel industry - flights, trains, coaches, hotels and holidays
  • Concerts and theatre performances
  • Cinemas - individual seat selection
  • Sporting events - stadium seating

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages to the customer
  • Can book 24 hours a day from anywhere, without travelling to a ticket office or queuing
  • Easy to compare prices, times and seats, and to see exactly which seats are free
  • Confirmation and e-tickets arrive immediately, and bookings can be changed online
  • Often cheaper, because the seller's costs are lower
Disadvantages to the customer
  • Needs a device, an internet connection and the confidence to use it
  • Personal and card details could be intercepted or hacked
  • Booking fees are often added, and popular events sell out in seconds
  • No help from a person if something goes wrong; a website crash or double payment is hard to resolve
Advantages to the company
  • No double bookings, because the database is updated in real time
  • Fewer staff and no need for expensive high-street offices, so costs fall
  • Sells worldwide, 24 hours a day
  • Customer data can be analysed to target advertising and set prices
Disadvantages to the company
  • Expensive to set up and to keep secure
  • If the system goes down, no bookings can be taken at all
  • Customers can compare prices instantly, which forces margins down
  • Responsible in law for protecting customers' personal data
Check your understanding
Explain how an online booking system prevents two people booking the same cinema seat.
As soon as a customer selects the seat, the record for that seat is flagged as unavailable in real time, so it disappears from the list other customers can see. Only when payment succeeds is it marked as fully booked; if the customer abandons the booking, the flag is released.
6.6

Banking Applications

Automatic Teller Machines (ATM)

Uses of an ATM
  • Withdrawing cash
  • Depositing cash or cheques
  • Checking the account balance
  • Printing mini statements
  • Paying bills
  • Transferring money between accounts

How a cash withdrawal works

  • The card is inserted and the chip is read to identify the account.
  • The customer keys in the PIN, which is checked against the card.
  • The customer selects the amount; the ATM contacts the bank's computer.
  • The bank checks the account balance, any daily withdrawal limit and whether the card has been reported stolen.
  • If all checks pass, the cash is dispensed, the account is debited and the card returned.
Advantages
  • Cash is available 24 hours a day, including when the bank is closed
  • Machines are in convenient places, and abroad as well as at home
  • Queues in branches are shorter, and the bank needs fewer cashiers
Disadvantages
  • Risk of shoulder surfing, card cloning and skimming devices
  • Robbery is a risk when withdrawing cash in the street
  • Charges may be made, and machines can run out of cash or break down
  • Fewer bank branches and jobs; not helpful for complex enquiries

Ways of paying

Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT)
Money is moved electronically from one account to another with no cash or paper changing hands. Used for salaries, direct debits and standing orders. It is fast, cheap and secure, and there is an automatic record - but an error in the account number sends the money to the wrong place, and the system depends on the network being available.
Credit and debit card transactions
A debit card takes the money from the account immediately; a credit card borrows from the card company, to be repaid later. The card is read by chip and PIN, contactless or online. Convenient and safer than carrying cash, and purchases are protected - but credit cards charge interest, encourage overspending, and card details can be stolen and used fraudulently.
Cheques
A written instruction to a bank to pay a stated sum from the writer's account. Useful where electronic payment is not possible and it gives a paper record - but cheques are slow to clear, can bounce if there are insufficient funds, are easily lost or forged, and are now rarely accepted.
Internet banking
The customer manages the account through a website or app: checking balances, viewing statements, transferring money, paying bills and setting up direct debits.

Internet banking - advantages and disadvantages

Advantages
  • Available 24 hours a day from anywhere, with no travelling and no queuing
  • Easy to compare accounts and interest rates between banks
  • Transactions and statements are instant, and payments can be automated
  • Banks save money on branches and staff, so they can offer better interest rates
Disadvantages
  • Risk of phishing, pharming and hacking, and of accounts being emptied
  • Needs a device, an internet connection and some ICT confidence
  • Branch closures affect those who cannot bank online, and personal contact is lost
  • Cash and cheques still cannot be paid in from home
  • Job losses among bank staff
Check your understanding
Describe the checks a bank's computer makes before an ATM dispenses cash.
It checks that the PIN matches the card, that the card has not been reported lost or stolen and has not expired, that there are sufficient funds or an agreed overdraft in the account, and that the daily withdrawal limit has not been exceeded. It also checks that the machine holds enough cash.
State two differences between a credit card and a debit card.
A debit card takes the money straight from the customer's own account, while a credit card borrows money from the card company to be repaid later. A credit card charges interest if the balance is not paid in full, and usually offers extra purchase protection; a debit card does not create a debt.

Computers in Medicine

Information systems in medicine

Patient records
  • Characteristics: a database holding personal details, medical history, allergies, test results, scans, treatments and appointments
  • Access is controlled by user id and password, and the data is encrypted
  • Records can be shared instantly between doctors, hospitals and specialists
Uses
  • Looking up a patient's history during a consultation
  • Booking appointments and tracking test results
  • Alerting staff automatically to allergies and drug interactions
  • Producing statistics on treatments and outbreaks
Advantages
  • Records are found instantly and can be read anywhere, even in an emergency
  • No lost or misfiled paper records, and no problems with handwriting
  • Automatic warnings about allergies reduce mistakes
  • Backups protect against loss, and data can be analysed for research
Disadvantages
  • Highly sensitive data could be hacked or disclosed
  • Expensive to set up, and staff need training
  • Everything stops if the system or power fails
  • Errors entered into the record spread quickly to every user
Pharmacy records
  • Characteristics: a database of medicines dispensed to each patient, linked to stock levels
Uses
  • Checking a new prescription against medicines the patient already takes
  • Warning of drug interactions and of prescriptions issued too often
  • Automatic reordering of stock, and tracking expiry dates and batch numbers
  • Printing labels and dosage instructions accurately

3D printers in medicine

  • Prosthetics - artificial limbs, hands and facial parts printed to fit an individual exactly, quickly and far more cheaply than traditional methods.
  • Tissue engineering - printing layers of living cells onto a scaffold to grow replacement tissue such as skin or cartilage.
  • Artificial blood vessels - printed vessels used in surgery and in testing, reducing the need for donors.
  • Customised medicines - tablets printed with a dose and a release rate matched to the individual patient, and several drugs combined into one tablet.
  • Surgeons also print models of a patient's organs from scan data to plan and rehearse an operation.
Advantages
  • Items are customised to the individual patient, so they fit and work better
  • Made quickly and locally, cutting waiting times
  • Much cheaper than traditional manufacture for one-off items
  • Reduces dependence on donors and on animal testing
Disadvantages
  • Printers, materials and trained staff are expensive
  • Printing is slow, and printed items may be less durable
  • Ethical concerns about printing living tissue
  • Regulation and testing of printed medicines is difficult
Check your understanding
Give two benefits of electronic patient records over paper records in an emergency.
The record can be retrieved instantly from any hospital, so staff who have never met the patient can see their history at once. The system also warns automatically about allergies and drug interactions, and there is no risk of a misfiled record or unreadable handwriting.
Explain one advantage of 3D printing a prosthetic hand for a growing child.
The hand can be customised exactly to the child from a scan and reprinted cheaply and quickly as the child grows, whereas a traditionally made prosthetic is expensive and slow to replace.
6.8

Expert Systems

What an expert system is

An expert system is software that stores the knowledge of human experts in a particular field and uses it to give advice, diagnose problems and suggest solutions - so that a non-expert can obtain expert-level help.
Uses
  • Mineral prospecting - deciding where to drill
  • Car engine fault diagnosis
  • Medical diagnosis
  • Chess games
  • Financial planning
  • Route scheduling for delivery vehicles
  • Plant and animal identification

Components of an expert system

User interface
The screens through which the user answers questions and receives the advice. Questions are usually simple, with yes/no or multiple-choice answers, so a non-expert can use the system.
Knowledge base
A very large database of facts collected from human experts in the subject - for example every known symptom, mineral or fault.
Rules base
A set of IF ... THEN ... rules that describe how the facts relate to each other and how conclusions are drawn from them.
Inference engine
The 'brain' of the system. It compares the user's answers with the knowledge base using the rules base, working out which questions to ask next and which conclusions are supported, each with a probability.
Explanation system
Tells the user how the conclusion was reached and how likely it is to be correct, so the advice can be checked and trusted.
A knowledge base editor is sometimes also listed: it is the tool experts use to add to and update the knowledge base and rules base.

How an expert system produces a solution

  • The user is asked a question through the user interface and gives an answer.
  • The inference engine searches the knowledge base and applies the rules base to that answer.
  • Based on the result, the next question is chosen, narrowing the possibilities each time.
  • When enough information has been gathered, the system outputs one or more possible solutions, each with a probability.
  • The explanation system states the reasoning behind each suggestion.
Advantages
  • Provides consistent answers - it never has an off day or forgets something
  • Expertise is available 24 hours a day, anywhere, including remote areas
  • Cheaper than employing a team of human experts, and it never retires
  • Holds more knowledge than any single person could remember
  • The reasoning is explained, so the user can check it
Disadvantages
  • Cannot use common sense or take account of anything outside the knowledge base
  • Very expensive and time-consuming to set up, and it must be kept up to date
  • The advice is only as good as the knowledge and rules put in - errors are repeated every time
  • Users may accept the answer without question; there is no human judgement or sympathy
Check your understanding
Name the component that decides which question to ask next, and explain how it works.
The inference engine. It compares the user's answers with the facts in the knowledge base using the IF...THEN statements in the rules base, eliminates possibilities and chooses the question that will narrow the remaining options fastest.
Why is the explanation system important in medical diagnosis?
It tells the doctor how the conclusion was reached and how probable it is, so the advice can be checked against the doctor's own judgement rather than being followed blindly. That matters because the system has no common sense and only knows what is in its knowledge base.
6.9

Computers in the Retail Industry

Point of sale (POS) terminals

Automatic stock control - the sequence to learn

  • The bar code is scanned at the POS terminal and the product number is read.
  • The number is searched for in the stock file on the supermarket computer.
  • The price and description are sent back to the till and printed on the receipt.
  • The stock level for that item is reduced by one.
  • The new stock level is compared with the re-order level.
  • If the stock level is less than or equal to the re-order level, the item is automatically re-ordered from the supplier.
  • When the order arrives the stock file is updated again.
Advantages
  • Stock levels are always up to date, so shelves are rarely empty
  • Faster checkouts and fewer pricing errors
  • Prices are changed in one place, not on every item
  • Sales data shows what sells and when, helping planning and special offers
  • Fewer staff needed for stock-taking
Disadvantages
  • Expensive to install and maintain
  • If the system fails, the shop may be unable to trade
  • Job losses among checkout and stock staff
  • Stock file errors, theft and damage mean a physical stock check is still needed

EFTPOS terminals

EFTPOS - electronic funds transfer at point of sale - is a POS terminal that also takes the payment electronically, transferring money from the customer's bank to the shop's bank.

What happens during a chip and PIN payment

  • The card is inserted and the chip is read; the terminal checks the card is valid and has not expired.
  • The customer keys in the PIN, which is compared with the PIN stored on the chip.
  • The terminal contacts the bank's computer and sends the card details and the amount.
  • The bank checks the card has not been reported stolen and that there are sufficient funds or credit.
  • If authorised, the customer's account is debited and the supermarket's account is credited, and a receipt is printed.
  • If not authorised, the transaction is refused.

Contactless and NFC payment

  • Contactless cards are held near the reader and use radio waves; no PIN is needed below a set limit, which makes payment very fast.
  • Near Field Communication (NFC) lets a smartphone or smart watch act as the card, with the payment authorised by fingerprint or face recognition.
  • Risks: a lost contactless card can be used by anyone until it is cancelled, and card details could be read at very close range - which is why there is a value limit and an occasional PIN request.

Internet shopping

Advantages to the customer
  • Shop 24 hours a day from home, with no travel costs or queues
  • Easy to compare prices and read reviews, and a far wider choice including goods from abroad
  • Helpful for elderly, disabled or rural customers
  • Often cheaper, because the retailer's costs are lower
Disadvantages to the customer
  • Goods cannot be seen, touched or tried on before buying
  • Delivery charges and waiting time; returns can be difficult
  • Risk of fraud, hacking and fake websites; personal data must be given out
  • Needs a device, internet access and a payment card
  • Less social contact, and local shops close
Advantages to the retailer
  • Sells worldwide without expensive high-street premises
  • Fewer staff, so lower overheads
  • Customer data can be analysed to target advertising
  • Open 24 hours a day
Disadvantages to the retailer
  • Cost of building and securing the website, and of delivery and returns
  • Customers can compare prices instantly, cutting profit margins
  • Risk of hacking and of losing customer data, with legal responsibility for it
  • No trading at all if the website goes down
Check your understanding
Describe how the supermarket computer automatically re-orders stock.
Each time an item is scanned, its stock level is reduced by one. The new level is compared with the item's re-order level; if it is less than or equal to it, an order for the re-order quantity is sent automatically to the supplier. The stock file is updated again when the delivery arrives.
Give one security advantage and one security risk of contactless payment.
Advantage: the card never leaves the customer's hand and the PIN is not typed in, so it cannot be observed by shoulder surfing. Risk: if the card is lost or stolen, anyone can use it for payments below the limit until it is cancelled.

Recognition Systems

Optical recognition

Optical Mark Recognition (OMR), bar codes and QR codes
  • OMR detects the position of a mark on a pre-printed form.
  • Bar codes hold a product number as lines of varying width, read by laser.
  • QR codes hold much more data in a two-dimensional matrix, read by a smartphone camera from any angle.
Uses
  • School registers and attendance sheets
  • Multiple-choice examination papers and questionnaires
  • Bar codes for stock control, libraries and parcels
  • QR codes for advertising, tickets, menus and payment links
Advantages
  • Very fast and accurate compared with typing data in
  • Cheap to print, and fewer staff are needed
  • QR codes hold more data and still work when partly damaged
Disadvantages
  • OMR forms must be designed and printed very accurately, and creased forms jam
  • Bar codes need line of sight and are unreadable if damaged
  • A malicious QR code can send a user to a fake website
Optical Character Recognition (OCR) and ANPR
  • OCR converts scanned printed or handwritten characters into editable text.
  • Automatic number plate recognition (ANPR) is OCR applied to vehicle number plates.

How ANPR works

  • A sensor detects a vehicle approaching and triggers the camera.
  • An image of the number plate is captured, and the area of the plate is located.
  • The image is converted to greyscale and the contrast adjusted so the characters stand out.
  • OCR converts each character into text, which is stored in a database with the time.
  • On exit the plate is read again and the charge calculated, or the plate is checked against a list of permitted or stolen vehicles.
Uses
  • Car park entry, exit and charging
  • Toll roads and congestion charging
  • Average speed enforcement and police checks on stolen vehicles
  • Automated border control reading passports
Advantages
  • No tickets or barriers needed, so traffic flows freely
  • Fewer staff and lower running costs
  • Automatic record of every vehicle, useful as evidence
Disadvantages
  • Dirty, damaged or non-standard plates are misread
  • Bad weather and poor light reduce accuracy
  • Records of every journey raise privacy concerns

RFID and NFC

Radio Frequency Identification (RFID)
  • A reader powers a tag by radio waves and the tag returns its data - no line of sight needed
  • Uses: tracking stock, passports, automobiles, contactless payment, livestock, library books
  • Many tags can be read at once and at a distance, and tags are robust
  • But tags cost more than bar codes, signals can be blocked by metal or liquid, and tags can be read without the owner knowing
Near Field Communication (NFC)
  • A short-range form of RFID working over a few centimetres only
  • Uses: payment using a smartphone or watch, transport ticketing, pairing devices, reading smart tags
  • Very fast and convenient; the short range and device authentication make interception difficult
  • But the device needs power, and a lost phone or card could be misused

Biometric recognition

Biometrics identify a person by measuring a unique physical or behavioural characteristic, comparing it with a stored template.
MethodHow it worksTypical benefits and problems
Face recognitionMeasures distances between facial featuresNon-intrusive and quick; affected by lighting, ageing, glasses and facial hair
Iris recognitionScans the unique pattern of the coloured ring of the eyeVery high accuracy; equipment is expensive and users may dislike an eye scan
Retina recognitionScans the pattern of blood vessels at the back of the eyeExtremely accurate; intrusive, slow and affected by some illnesses

Advantages
  • Very difficult to forge, share, lose or forget
  • No card or password to carry or remember
  • Provides a clear record of exactly who accessed what and when
Disadvantages
  • Equipment is expensive and can be intrusive
  • Systems can produce false rejections and false acceptances
  • Injury, illness or ageing can stop the system recognising a person
  • If the stored biometric data is stolen it can never be changed, unlike a password
Check your understanding
Describe how an ANPR system charges a driver in a car park.
A sensor triggers a camera as the car enters; the image of the plate is captured, converted to greyscale with the contrast adjusted, and OCR converts the characters into text, which is stored with the arrival time. On exit the plate is read again, the time in the car park is calculated and the charge worked out; the barrier opens once payment is confirmed.
Why is a stolen fingerprint template more serious than a stolen password?
A password can be changed as soon as it is compromised, but a fingerprint is a permanent physical characteristic that can never be changed, so once its template is stolen the person's biometric identity is permanently at risk.

Satellite Systems

Global Positioning System (GPS) and satellite navigation

How GPS works

  • Satellites orbiting the Earth continuously transmit their position and the exact time.
  • The GPS receiver picks up signals from several satellites and measures how long each signal took to arrive.
  • From these times it calculates its distance from each satellite and works out its own position - a process called trilateration.
  • Signals from at least three satellites are needed for a position, and four for altitude as well; more satellites give greater accuracy.
  • A satellite navigation system adds stored maps, so the position is shown on a road map and a route is calculated with spoken directions.
Uses
  • In-car and phone navigation, and route planning for delivery fleets
  • Tracking vehicles, ships, aircraft and stolen goods
  • Surveying, farming, walking and sailing
  • Emergency services locating a caller
Advantages
  • The driver does not need to read a paper map, which is safer
  • Routes can be recalculated automatically, avoiding traffic and road closures
  • Estimated arrival times, speed limits and nearby services are shown
  • Works almost anywhere in the world
Disadvantages
  • Out-of-date maps send drivers down unsuitable or closed roads
  • The signal is lost in tunnels, between tall buildings and in deep valleys
  • Drivers may follow instructions without thinking, causing accidents
  • Location data can be used to track people, raising privacy concerns

Geographic Information Systems (GIS)

A GIS combines maps with layers of data about a place, so that information can be analysed and displayed geographically.
Uses
  • Emergency planning - showing flood risk, evacuation routes and where crews are
  • Town planning and choosing sites for new shops, schools or wind farms
  • Utilities mapping the position of pipes and cables underground
  • Farming - mapping soil, yield and irrigation across a field
  • Tracking the spread of disease or pollution
Advantages
  • Very large amounts of data can be understood quickly when shown on a map
  • Layers can be combined to reveal patterns that a table would hide
  • Supports better planning decisions and faster emergency response
Disadvantages
  • Expensive software and a lot of accurate data are needed
  • Skilled operators and training are required
  • Conclusions are wrong if the underlying data is out of date or inaccurate

Media communication systems

Satellite television
  • Programmes are beamed up to a satellite and broadcast back down over a wide area
  • Received by a dish that must have a clear line of sight to the satellite
  • Reaches remote areas with no cable, and offers many channels in high definition
  • But heavy rain or snow can interrupt the signal, a dish and subscription are needed, and there is a slight transmission delay
Satellite phones
  • Connect directly to a satellite instead of a mobile phone mast
  • Work anywhere in the world, including at sea, in deserts and in disaster zones
  • Essential for shipping, expeditions, aid agencies and emergency services
  • But handsets and calls are expensive, the phone needs a clear view of the sky, and there is a noticeable delay
Check your understanding
Explain why a satellite navigation system may give the wrong directions.
The stored maps may be out of date, so new roads, one-way systems or closures are not shown. The satellite signal can also be lost in tunnels or between tall buildings, so the receiver cannot calculate an accurate position.
A rescue team works in a remote mountain region. Explain why they carry a satellite phone rather than a mobile phone.
A mobile phone needs to be within range of a mobile phone mast, and there are none in remote mountains. A satellite phone communicates directly with a satellite, so it works anywhere with a clear view of the sky.
