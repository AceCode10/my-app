Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 · Topic 5

The Effects of Using IT

Microprocessors are now built into the devices we live with - ovens, doorbells, watches, cars. This topic asks you to weigh up what that does to the way people live, and to know the health problems caused by spending long hours at a computer, with a cause and a prevention strategy for each one.

Syllabus coverage: 5.1 Microprocessor-controlled devices · 5.2 Potential health problems related to the prolonged use of IT equipment
5.1

Microprocessor-Controlled Devices

What a microprocessor-controlled device is

A microprocessor-controlled device contains an embedded processor that monitors sensors and controls the device automatically. A smart device goes further: it also connects to a network so it can be monitored and controlled remotely, usually from a phone app.
  • Monitoring - the system reads sensors and reports, but does not change anything by itself (for example a fitness tracker or a home security camera).
  • Controlling - the system reads sensors and acts on the readings using actuators (for example a smart thermostat turning the heating on).
Exam tip: questions in this section almost always ask for positive and negative effects. Plan your answer with two columns before you start writing, and make sure your points are about the effect on people, not about how the device works.

Devices in the home monitoring & controlling

Examples you can use

  • Smart thermostats and heating systems; smart lighting
  • Washing machines, ovens, microwaves and dishwashers with programmes
  • Fridges that track contents and suggest shopping lists
  • Burglar alarms, smart doorbells and security cameras
  • Robot vacuum cleaners and lawnmowers
  • Fitness trackers and smart watches

Impact on lifestyle

Positive effects
  • People are freed from routine chores, which the device does automatically
  • Devices can be controlled remotely, so the heating can be on before you arrive home
  • Devices run at the most efficient settings, so energy bills and waste fall
  • People no longer need to stay at home while a job is done
  • Greatly helps elderly and disabled people to live independently
Negative effects
  • People can become lazy and reliant on the technology, and household skills such as cooking are lost
  • Devices are expensive to buy and to repair, and become obsolete quickly
  • They still need to be programmed, set up and maintained
  • They waste energy if left on standby, and add to electronic waste
  • Everything stops working in a power cut or if the network fails

Impact on leisure time

Positive effects
  • More free time, because chores are done automatically
  • Free time can be used for hobbies, family or exercise
  • Smart entertainment devices give instant access to music, films and games at home
Negative effects
  • The extra free time is often spent sitting in front of a screen
  • People may spend leisure time indoors rather than being active outside
  • Being always contactable means work can intrude on leisure time

Impact on physical fitness

Positive effects
  • Fitness trackers and smart watches monitor steps, heart rate and sleep, and encourage people to exercise
  • Free time released by smart devices can be used for sport and exercise
  • Online exercise classes and app-based coaching make training easier to access
Negative effects
  • Less physical effort is needed around the home, so people take less exercise
  • A more sedentary lifestyle can lead to obesity and heart problems
  • Food can be ordered and delivered with no need to walk to the shops

Impact on security of data

Positive effects
  • Smart alarms and cameras improve the security of the home itself and alert the owner immediately
  • Data can be encrypted, and access protected by passwords and two-factor authentication
Negative effects
  • Smart devices are connected to the internet, so they can be hacked
  • Devices collect personal data about daily routines, which reveals when a house is empty
  • Many devices ship with weak default passwords and are rarely updated
  • Data is stored by the manufacturer, and may be sold on or leaked

Impact on the degree of social interaction

Positive effects
  • Time saved can be spent with family and friends
  • Smart devices let people keep in contact with distant relatives by video call
  • Housebound people are less isolated because they can stay connected
Negative effects
  • People shop, bank and are entertained at home, so they meet fewer people face to face
  • Family members may each use their own device rather than spend time together
  • Social skills can suffer, and some people become socially isolated

Monitoring and controlling transport

Examples you can use

  • Satellite navigation and live traffic routing
  • Automatic number plate recognition for tolls, car parks and speed enforcement
  • Engine management systems, anti-lock brakes and automatic emergency braking
  • Public transport tracking and smart ticketing
  • Autonomous (self-driving) vehicles

Transport safety

Positive effects
  • Sensors and processors react far faster than a human driver, so accidents are avoided
  • Systems never get tired, distracted or emotional, and do not drink
  • Vehicles keep a safe distance, stay in lane and obey speed limits automatically
  • Traffic flows more smoothly, cutting congestion and pollution
  • Journeys can be planned around live traffic, saving time and fuel
Negative effects
  • A hardware fault or software bug can cause a serious accident
  • Drivers may become over-reliant and lose their driving skills
  • Systems cannot always cope with unexpected events, bad weather or unclear road markings
  • Responsibility for an accident is unclear - driver, owner or manufacturer
  • Job losses for professional drivers
  • Systems are expensive to install and maintain

Security of data in transport systems

Positive effects
  • Tracking data helps recover stolen vehicles and provides evidence after an accident
  • Immobilisers and keyless systems make vehicles harder to steal
Negative effects
  • Vehicles are connected, so they can be hacked and even controlled remotely
  • Journey data shows exactly where a person travels and when, which is a privacy risk
  • Data held by manufacturers and toll operators can be stolen or misused
  • Keyless entry signals can be intercepted and relayed to steal a car
Autonomous vehicles in one line: they use cameras, radar, lidar and GPS to sense the road, a processor to decide, and actuators to steer, accelerate and brake - removing human error but introducing software risk.
Check your understanding
Give two positive and two negative effects of fitting smart devices throughout a home.
  • Positive: chores are done automatically, giving the family more leisure time; devices can be controlled remotely and run efficiently, so energy costs fall.
  • Negative: the devices are connected to the internet so they can be hacked, and they collect data showing when the house is empty; people do less physical work and may become less fit and more reliant on the technology.
A car manufacturer claims autonomous vehicles will be safer. Give one argument for and one against.
For: the system reacts far faster than a human and never becomes tired, distracted or intoxicated, which removes the main cause of accidents. Against: a software bug or sensor failure could cause an accident, and the system may not cope with unexpected situations or poor weather.
5.2

Health Problems from Prolonged Use of IT Equipment

How to answer these questions

For each health problem you must be able to give the cause and a strategy for preventing it. Marks are lost when students give a cause where a prevention is asked for, so read the command word carefully.

Safety is not the same as health. Electrocution, fire and tripping over cables are safety issues and belong to Topic 8. This section is about health problems caused by long periods of use.

Repetitive strain injury (RSI)

RSI - damage to the joints, tendons and muscles of the hands, wrists and arms
Causes
  • Repeated clicking of a mouse and long periods of typing
  • Holding the wrists in an awkward or unsupported position
  • Gripping a mouse too tightly, and working without a break
  • Poor posture at a badly set up workstation
Prevention strategies
  • Use a wrist rest to support the wrists while typing or using the mouse
  • Take regular breaks and do exercises that stretch the hands and arms
  • Use an ergonomic keyboard and mouse, or voice input to cut down typing
  • Keep the forearms horizontal and adjust the chair so the wrists stay straight

Back and neck problems

Back and neck pain - damage to the spine and supporting muscles
Causes
  • Sitting in the same position for too long
  • Poor posture and slouching in the chair
  • A chair with no back support or of the wrong height
  • A monitor set too low, too high or off to one side, so the neck is twisted
Prevention strategies
  • Use a fully adjustable chair with lumbar support, set so the feet are flat on the floor
  • Use a footrest if the feet do not reach the floor comfortably
  • Position the monitor directly in front of the user at eye level
  • Take regular breaks, stand up and walk around, and use a sit-stand desk

Eye problems

Eye strain and dry, sore eyes
Causes
  • Staring at a screen for too long without looking away
  • Screen glare and reflections from windows or lights
  • A flickering, dirty or badly adjusted screen
  • Working in poor lighting, and blinking less than normal
Prevention strategies
  • Take regular breaks and look at a distant object to refocus the eyes
  • Fit an anti-glare screen filter, and use blinds to control daylight
  • Position the screen so windows and lights are not reflected in it
  • Keep the screen clean, set a comfortable brightness, and use a good quality flicker-free monitor
  • Have regular eye tests and wear the correct glasses

Headaches

Headaches and migraines
Causes
  • Eye strain from staring at the screen and from screen glare
  • Incorrect or uncorrected eyesight, forcing the eyes to work harder
  • Poor posture and tension in the neck and shoulders
  • Screen flicker, and working in a stuffy or badly lit room
Prevention strategies
  • Take regular breaks away from the screen
  • Use an anti-glare filter and correct the room lighting
  • Have regular eye tests and wear the correct glasses
  • Set up the workstation correctly and keep the room well ventilated

Check your understanding
A data entry clerk types for six hours a day and complains of pain in the wrists. Name the condition and give two ways the employer could reduce the risk.
The condition is repetitive strain injury (RSI). The employer could provide a wrist rest and an ergonomic keyboard so the wrists stay supported and straight, and require regular breaks away from the keyboard with stretching exercises. Voice recognition software would also cut the amount of typing.
Explain why an office worker sitting next to a large window may suffer headaches.
Sunlight causes glare and reflections on the screen, so the eyes work harder to read it. The resulting eye strain leads to headaches. Fitting blinds and an anti-glare filter, and repositioning the monitor so the window is not reflected, would prevent it.

  • I can tell a health issue apart from a safety issue.
